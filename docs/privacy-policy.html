Bluenest Bugshot — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2026

Bluenest Bugshot is an internal tool for Bluenest's own QA team. It lets a tester select a region of a web page, annotate it, and send it as a card to a Notion database the tester controls. This page explains exactly what data the extension touches and where it goes.

What the extension does with your data

The extension only captures a screenshot when you explicitly trigger it (clicking its toolbar icon, a keyboard shortcut, or its right-click menu). It never captures or transmits anything in the background or without that direct action.

Once captured, the screenshot and any annotations you add are processed entirely inside your browser (using the Canvas API) until you choose to send it. When you click Send, the following is sent directly from your browser to Notion's API (api.notion.com) to create a page in the Notion database you've connected:

  • The screenshot image, with your annotations flattened into it
  • The web page's URL and title at the time of capture
  • Your browser and OS (parsed from your browser's own user agent string), viewport size, and pixel ratio
  • The title, severity, and notes you typed into the form
  • The reporter name you set in the extension's Settings

None of this is sent anywhere except directly to Notion's own API, using your own Notion connection. Bluenest does not operate any server that receives, stores, or has access to your screenshots, QA card content, or Notion data.

Notion connection (sign-in)

To connect the extension to Notion, you either:

  • Use "Connect to Notion" (OAuth) — Notion's own authorization screen asks you to approve access and choose which pages/databases to share. A small backend service we operate (a Cloudflare Worker) exchanges the authorization code Notion provides for an access token and a refresh token, and stores that token pair, associated only with a random identifier your browser generates — never your name, email, or any identifying information. This service's only job is keeping that token valid; it never receives, reads, or stores your screenshots or QA card content, since those are sent directly from your browser to Notion, not through this service.
  • Or paste a Notion internal integration token manually — this is stored only in your browser's local extension storage and is never sent anywhere except directly to Notion's API.

What's stored, and where

  • In your browser (Chrome's local extension storage, never synced or sent elsewhere): your Notion connection token/identifier, your configured database, your reporter name, and your last-used annotation preferences.
  • On our Cloudflare Worker: only the OAuth token pair described above, keyed to a random per-install identifier — no screenshots, no QA card content, no identifying information.
  • Nowhere else. The extension includes no analytics, advertising, or tracking of any kind.

Data retention and deletion

A captured screenshot is held only in temporary browser session storage while you're editing it, and is cleared automatically once that's done. To remove your stored OAuth connection, disconnect in Notion directly (Notion's own Settings → Connections), or contact us to have the corresponding token pair deleted from the Worker.

Permissions this extension requests, and why

  • activeTab / scripting — to inject the region-selection overlay only on the tab you're actively using, only when you trigger a capture.
  • storage — to save your settings locally in your browser.
  • notifications — to show a system notification if a page can't be captured (e.g. Chrome's own internal pages).
  • contextMenus — for the right-click capture options.
  • identity — to open Notion's OAuth consent screen.
  • Host access to api.notion.com and our Cloudflare Worker — required to send QA cards to Notion and to manage your OAuth connection, respectively.
